`stable_worldmodel` is the library stable-envs is meant to feed. This note answers two questions with
measurements rather than with directory listings: **which of its environments can we render**, and
**what happens to the ones we cannot**.

The env list is enumerated LIVE — `import stable_worldmodel`, then read `gymnasium`'s registry
filtered to the `swm/` namespace — because the registry is built at import time and a family can
vanish silently: `stable_worldmodel/envs/__init__.py` ends with a bare `try: from
stable_worldmodel.envs import ale except ImportError: pass`, and `swm/ImagePositioning-v1` is
commented out of the file entirely. Reading the directory names gives a different, wrong answer to
both. Reproduce with `scratch/swm_registry.py`, which also records, per id, whether it constructs
here and what it puts in `info`.

**42 ids are registered. 40 are covered by stable-envs, 2 have no frame to render, and 0 are not.**
The 40 are every pixel-producing id: everything drawn in 2-D, all eleven DM Control ids, the 12
Fetch ids, both Craftax pixel ids, both OGBench manipulation ids, the OGBench mazes and the PyFlyt
rocket. The 2 without a frame are `swm/CraftaxClassicSymbolic-v1` and `swm/CraftaxSymbolic-v1`,
which are **intentionally renderer-free**: the upstream wrapper itself refuses, raising
`NotImplementedError: render is only supported for Pixels variants; symbolic envs do not produce a
frame` from `stable_worldmodel.envs.craftax.craftax:CraftaxClassicSymbolicWrapper` and
`:CraftaxSymbolicWrapper`. That is the env's own declaration, recorded verbatim by the live registry
probe, not a judgement made here.

Step 7 closed the last six gaps and took a seventh id from partial to full:
`swm/CraftaxClassicPixels-v1` (partial -> full), `swm/CraftaxPixels-v1`,
`swm/ManipulatorDMControl-v0`, `swm/QuadrupedDMControl-v0`, `swm/OGBCube-v0`, `swm/OGBScene-v0` and
`swm/PFRocketLanding-v0` — the last of which this note had recorded as a structural NO-GO.

### Three constants that were wrong on first reading

Recorded because each cost a measurement and each is invisible by eye. The full set is in the module
docstrings of `stable_envs/envs/navigation.py` (Step 9 merged `swm_nav.py` and `simple_point_maze.py`
into that module; the old filenames remain `sys.modules` aliases until Wave 3).

**The "agent dot" is a 41-pixel object with a nominal radius of 7.** `TwoRoomEnv` composites a
normalised Gaussian alpha mask over the whole frame and truncates to uint8, so a background of 255
becomes 254 wherever `1-a` is representably below 1.0 in float32. Measured on the official frame with
the agent at (60,112): **4,921 pixels touched** where a flat disc of radius 7 covers 149, **3,304 of
them at the single value 254**, and 102 distinct green levels along the ramp. Drawing a disc is wrong
over a 41-pixel radius and looks right.

**`exp` in float32 costs 40× the pixel disagreement.** The port is otherwise the reference's own
arithmetic, so the only remaining source of disagreement is the transcendental: CUDA's `expf` and the
CPU's differ by up to one ulp, and `.to(uint8)` then turns that into a whole pixel wherever
`255*(1-a)` sits within an ulp of an integer. Measured over 64 states per env:

| variant | TwoRoom | TwoRoomTarget | Piecewise | PiecewiseZones |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `exp` in float32 | 2,561 px, interior 1.0 | 4,993 px, interior 0.9999982 | 3,001 px, interior 0.9999988 | 672 px, interior 0.9999996 |
| **`exp` in float64, rounded once** | **61 px, interior 1.0** | **172 px, interior 1.0** | **193 px, interior 1.0** | **18 px, interior 1.0** |
| `dist2` *and* `exp` in float64 | 3,246 px, interior 0.9999976 | 5,913 px | 3,425 px | 798 px |

The third row is the informative one: widening the *argument* as well makes it worse, because the
reference's argument really is float32 and a more accurate input is a different input. Only the
transcendental is wrong in float32.

**Two alpha blends are separated by a uint8 quantisation.** `_render_frame` calls `_alpha_blend`
twice — target, then agent — and `_alpha_blend` ends in `.to(torch.uint8)`. Chaining the two in
float32 and quantising once is the obvious implementation. Measured on `TwoRoomTarget` against the
official frames: quantising between them leaves **172** disagreeing pixels, chaining leaves
**35,957**. All of it 1/255, none of it visible.

### Knobs the official envs declare and never read

Two, both found by moving the knob on the OFFICIAL env and diffing its own frames:

* `TwoRoom`'s **`wall.border_color`**: `_wall_and_door_masks` folds the four borders into
  `wall_mask` and `_render_frame` paints `wall_mask` with `wall.color`. Setting `border_color` to
  (255,255,255) and to (0,0,0) gives **max abs diff 0/255**. It is not declared here; a port that
  copies the leaf list inherits a dead knob and `check_variations_do_something` then fails on it.
* `Piecewise`'s **`zones.bias_*`**, and both envs' **`agent.speed`** and **`task.min_steps`**: these
  are dynamics or task definitions and change no pixel. They are carried as plain attributes
  (`env.speed`, `env.zone_bias`) so `step` can use them.

### Why five classes for three ids

`rendering.render_target` and `rendering.render_zones` both ship as **0**, and `SimplePointMazeEnv`
takes `show_goal`. Under the shipped defaults the target dot and the zone tints are not drawn, so
every knob describing them is inert and every state dimension carrying them is dead. There are three
ways out and only one of them is honest:

1. default the toggle on — makes "our defaults are the official appearance" false, and that identity
   is what every parity number is measured under;
2. declare the knobs anyway — `check_variations_do_something` fails, correctly;
3. **one class per configuration**, each with defaults that reproduce the official env *in that
   configuration*. This is what MiniGrid already does for top-down versus POV against one official
   id, so it is the repo's existing answer rather than a new one.

### One deliberate limitation: `render(size=)`

All five refuse any size but their native one, and this is not laziness. Every length in these
references is in OUTPUT PIXELS — a 4 px border, a door half-extent in pixels, a Gaussian sigma in
pixels, a wall at column 112, a 0.8 pt matplotlib spine at 100 dpi — with no world-unit description
underneath to re-sample at another rate. Rendering `TwoRoom` at 448 would not be the same picture
sampled more finely; it would be a different picture with a 4-pixel border on a 448-pixel canvas.
`stable_worldmodel`'s own pipeline resizes the frame afterwards with PIL, and that is the operation a
caller who wants another size should perform.

The consequence for the suite: `test_resolution_downsample_agrees_within_the_measured_bound` and
`test_render_size_does_not_leak_into_later_calls` both catch `ValueError` and skip, so these envs
need no entry in `DOWNSAMPLE_EXACT_FRAC`.

## SimplePointMaze: the first matplotlib reference

Worth its own section because the reference is an Agg figure rather than pygame, a tile atlas or
MuJoCo, and because everything about it had to be recovered by measurement.

**Gates** (`scratch/simple_point_maze_gates.py`): G3 render-twice **0/255**, G3 after a 20-step
rollout **0/255**, G1 second instance **0/255**. G2 is **8 bytes against 750,000** = 1/93,750, the
largest ratio in this repo. G4: the official renderer does **75 f/s**; ours does 26,301.

**The constants** (`scratch/simple_point_maze_calib.py`), each measured by constructing a frame in
which only the quantity of interest can be responsible for a pixel:

* the axes fill the figure exactly at **100 px per data unit** — `tight_layout(pad=0)` with no ticks
  leaves no margin. A wall at data `x∈[1,2], y∈[1,2]` lands on rows 300..399, columns 100..199, and
  three more probes agree, giving `col = 100x`, `row = 500 − 100y` to the pixel. So the scene is
  `world = (0,0,5,5)` with `flip_y` and nothing converts to pixels by hand;
* the axes **spine is asymmetric**: row 0 and column 0 are **pure black across their whole length**,
  rows/columns 1 and 499 are **(241,241,241)**, and the four places where two grey lines cross are
  **(227,227,227)**. Row/column 498 is plain background. Writing the top grey band one pixel out —
  the obvious reading of "a 1-pixel border" — costs **24,089 disagreeing pixels** across 48 frames,
  which is how the correct geometry was found;
* the spine paints **over** the walls (matplotlib zorder 2.5 against a patch's 1): a wall at
  `x∈[0,0.5]` starts at column **1**;
* the default wall layout is a constant of the family, drawn once from `default_rng(234232)` with
  **shapes before positions**; the parity script asserts our reproduction against the live reference
  rather than trusting it (max err **0.0**).

**The goal disc is not drawn, and the reason is worth recording.** `SimplePointMazeEnv` draws the
goal as `Circle(..., alpha=0.5)`. Two measured facts decide it:

1. *The compositing rule is closed-form and was solved.* Over 12 (background, colour) pairs = 36
   channels, `out = floor(bg + (c − bg)·(128 − ε)/255)` reproduces **36/36**. `floor(bg +
   (c−bg)·128/255)` gets 34/36 and `floor((c+bg)/2)` gets 27/36; the two channels that separate them
   are the ones where the product is an exact integer, where Agg's multiply-shift lands one below. A
   sweep over every integer alpha and five candidate rules put the best alternative at 27/36. And the
   env's own `constrain_fn` forbids the goal from overlapping a wall, so one pre-blend against the
   background is always sufficient. **An exact port is available.**
2. *A translucent primitive cannot satisfy `check_colors_apply_exactly`.* That check sets each
   declared RGB knob to a literal colour and requires the frame to contain that colour somewhere. A
   disc composited at 128/255 never puts its own colour on the canvas — neither does the official
   env: `goal.color = (17,33,200)` over the default white background produces **(135,143,227)**.

So the registered class is the `show_goal=False` configuration. **This is the one place where a
shared check, rather than the criterion or our renderer, is what stands in the way**, and the fix is
small and belongs in a file this work does not own: `check_colors_apply_exactly` in
`stable_envs/testing/conformance.py` would need a way for an env to declare a leaf as composited —
for example a class-level `translucent_color_paths` that the check consults and, for those leaves,
asserts that the knob *changes* the frame rather than that it appears in it. With that,
`SimplePointMazeGoal` is an afternoon: the blend rule above, one extra disc primitive, and the goal's
three knobs.

## Fetch (gymnasium-robotics)

12 registered ids over 4 distinct scenes (`Reach`, `Push`, `Slide`, `PickAndPlace`; the `Dense` and
`Dict` variants change the reward and the observation container, not a pixel). All 12 construct and
render here at 224×224.

Gates measured (`scratch/swm_gaps_gates.py`, `scratch/fetch_g1.py`):

| gate | result | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **G3** decoder stability | `nq/nv/ngeom/nmesh/nbody/ntex/nmat` **identical across 3 resets** for all four scenes: Reach 15/15/23/**18**/32/**2**/11, the other three 22/21/24/**18**/33/**2**/11 | **pass** — unlike RoboCasa, the model does not change shape per episode |
| **G1** purity | `qpos + qvel` alone: **29–138/255**. `qpos + qvel + mocap_pos + mocap_quat + goal`: **0/255, 100% of pixels exact**, all four scenes | **pass**, once the state is complete |
| **G2** compression | 40 float64 (Reach) / 53 (others) against 150,528 B → **1/470 and 1/355** | **pass** |
| **G4** economics | official EGL renderer **641–758 f/s** at 224px; the decode baseline at 224² is 6,762 f/s/core | a GPU port must beat ~6.8 k f/s to be worth writing |

The G1 line is the one to read carefully. The first probe restored `qpos` and `qvel` and measured
29–138/255, which reads exactly like a purity failure. It is not one: `MujocoFetchEnv` drives the
gripper through a **mocap body**, whose pose is not in the physics state, and stores the target as a
site position outside it too. Adding both gives 0/255. The honest form of this result is "the
render-relevant state is 40–53 floats and here is which ones", not "Fetch fails G1" — and the same
trap is waiting for anyone who checkpoints a Fetch episode by saving `qpos`.

**Verdict: GO, and LANDED — see [`fetch.md`](fetch.md).** All four gates clear, and the renderer that
was missing is a baked static plate (floor, table, skybox: 91% of the frame, exact by construction)
plus `mujoco_warp`'s batch raytracer for the 18 robot meshes, which is the mover set no plate can
absorb because every arm joint is in `qpos`. Interior agreement is **0.9988–0.9990** with a declared
`parity_note`, whole-frame exactness 0.908–0.928, at **6.19×** the official renderer at 480 px and
**6.47×** at 224 (16,202 f/s, which clears the 6,762 f/s/core decode baseline in the G4 row above by
2.4×).

Two of the reasons recorded above for not building it did not survive measurement, and both are kept
here because they were the stated blocker:

* **`ntex = 2` was never the obstacle.** One texture is the skybox; the other, `texture_block`, is
  declared in `shared.xml` and used by **no material**. Every Fetch material is flat with
  `reflectance=0`, so `texture_gaps()` is empty on all four models and MJWarp's PLANE/MESH-only texgen
  limitation never fires. Fetch is a flat-material scene — the case [`backend3d.md`](backend3d.md)
  measures at 98.9% interior-exact — and not a case sitting between the two measured ones. The alpha
  bug was not its blocker either: Fetch has *zero* geoms at alpha 0. It has four at alpha 0.7 and 0.1,
  which is the case with no correct handling at all, and §5 of `fetch.md` measures what excluding them
  costs.
* **The G1 line above needs narrowing, not widening.** `qpos + qvel + mocap + goal` giving 0/255 is a
  fact about the reference. The RENDER-relevant state is **18 floats (Reach) or 25**: `qvel` moves no
  pixel, and `mocap` reaches nothing but four TRANSLUCENT marker geoms — 1.2–1.5% of all pixels and
  **0.000002 of interior pixels**, which no alpha-blind renderer can draw and which are therefore
  excluded and declared. So G2 here is 1/9,600 (Reach) and 1/6,900, not 1/470.

The single largest fidelity term was on neither list: `mujoco_warp` quantises float to uint8 by
TRUNCATION where OpenGL rounds, worth interior **0.791 → 0.9988** on its own. It is now
`stable_envs.render.mjwarp.gl_rounding()`, and every MJWarp caller in this repo wants it.

One deployment consequence, stated because it is visible in the registry: `mujoco_warp` needs
`mujoco >= 3.11` and this suite's interpreter carries 3.9, so `stable_envs/envs/fetch.py` raises
`ImportError` at module scope there and the 12 ids are absent from *that* registry with the reason in
`import_errors()`. They were measured in `~/venvs/se-fetch`, a `--system-site-packages` venv that
shadows only `mujoco`; `pytest -k Fetch` there is **405 passed, 76 skipped, 0 failed**.

## DM Control

12 registered ids. When this note was written, **none of them constructed in this environment**: every
one raised `AttributeError: 'MjModel' object has no attribute 'flex_bandwidth'`, a version skew between
the installed `dm_control` and `mujoco`.

> **Update (2026-08-10).** That skew is closed and the family is no longer a NO-GO. `dm_control` 1.0.41
> generates its named-index table from a `mujoco` that still had `flex_bandwidth`; **3.9.0 removed it**,
> and no `dm_control` release works with 3.9. The pin is `mujoco==3.11.0` + `dm_control==1.0.44`, in
> `/mnt/vast/home/randall.balestriero/venvs/dmc` (the shared env is left on mujoco 3.9, because moving
> it would move every OGBench parity number too). With it loaded, **a frame is exactly `f(qpos)` on all
> 17 domains probed** — zero pixels change under re-render, `qvel × 100`, or reaching the same `qpos`
> through a different history, with no dead dimension anywhere — and five fixed-camera cells now ship
> at **interior parity 1.0, no tolerance relaxed**. See [`dmcontrol.md`](dmcontrol.md).

What *can* be established from the suite XML alone is G3, and it is decisive for the 2-D path. Every
2-D domain includes `common/materials.xml` and `common/skybox.xml`, and those files say:

```xml
<texture name="grid" type="2d" builtin="checker" rgb1=".1 .2 .3" rgb2=".2 .3 .4" width="300" height="300" mark="edge"/>
<material name="grid" texture="grid" texrepeat="1 1" texuniform="true" reflectance=".2"/>
<texture name="skybox" type="skybox" builtin="gradient" rgb1=".4 .6 .8" rgb2="0 0 0" width="800" height="800" mark="random"/>
<quality shadowsize="2048"/>
```

A 300×300 checkerboard **with reflectance**, a gradient skybox with random marks, a 2048 shadow map,
and perspective cameras. The decoder is not a finite set of flat primitives, so the 2-D IR cannot
express it — confirmed for 19 suite XMLs (`acrobot`, `ball_in_cup`, `cartpole`, `cheetah`, `dog`,
`finger`, `fish`, `hopper`, `humanoid`, `humanoid_CMU`, `lqr`, `manipulator`, `pendulum`,
`point_mass`, `quadruped`, `reacher`, `stacker`, `swimmer`, `walker`).

**Verdict: NO-GO for the 2-D path, unchanged. GO on the 3-D path, and it landed.** The paragraph that
used to stand here argued the 3-D path down as well, on the grounds that `reflectance=.2` on a
*textured* checkerboard was "precisely the case `backend3d.md` measures at 7.6% exact". That premise is
withdrawn twice over. First, the 7.6% was MJWarp's alpha bug, not its texture path (see the correction
box under [Fetch](#fetch-gymnasium-robotics)). Second, and more to the point, **MJWarp was measured
directly on this suite and is not the renderer these envs use**: it lands at 19–31% exact / MAE
3.1–4.6 on the side-camera domains and 0.5% / MAE 23 on the top-down ones, which is the lightly-textured
band rather than a failure, and still 10–20× short of the bar.

What the two remaining XML facts actually cost is now measured rather than inferred. `reflectance=.2`
and `shadowsize=2048` do mean a moving geom is mirrored in the floor and darkens floor pixels it does
not cover, so a static plate is not the frame — but both are recoverable from **two extra bakes**: one
with the shadow-casting light's diffuse and specular zeroed (which IS the colour of a surface that
light does not reach), one with `mat_reflectance = 0` (which inverts MuJoCo's own
`reflectance·mirror + (1−reflectance)·surface`). Five cells ship at 97.7–99.8% of all pixels exact,
mean 0.02–0.37/255, with every disagreeing pixel inside the mover pass. The remaining domains are held
back by a `trackcom` camera, a per-episode target, or a spatial tendon — not by the floor and not by
the sky.

## Rocket landing (PyFlyt / PyBullet)

`swm/PFRocketLanding-v0` **does not construct here**: `ModuleNotFoundError: No module named 'cvxpy'`,
raised from the family's `__init__` importing its expert policy. So no pixel was measured, and this
verdict is structural and labelled as such.

Read off the source, `RocketLandingEnv`:

* it is a **PyBullet** scene rendered at 480×480 through PyFlyt's camera — a third renderer, with no
  batched backend anywhere in this repo, and a perspective camera over URDF **mesh** assets;
* it **writes a new URDF to a temporary file on every reset**: `_modify_rocket_urdf` parses
  `PyFlyt/models/vehicles/rocket/rocket.urdf`, rewrites the `yellow` / `grey` / `black` material
  colours from the variation space, and writes the result through `tempfile.NamedTemporaryFile`;
  `_modify_pad_urdf` does the same for the landing pad.

The second point is the RoboCasa signature exactly: the decoder is regenerated per episode from an
asset library rather than being a constant of the family. It is a milder case — the *geometry* is
constant and only the material colours change, so a port could compile the URDF once and treat colour
as a per-environment tensor — but it fails the gate as the env is written, and the family would need
the 3-D path it does not have.

**Verdict: NO-GO for now, on G3 as written plus the absence of a PyBullet backend.** Not pixel-
verified; `cvxpy` and `PyFlyt` would have to be installed first, and neither was installed here.

## ImagePositioning (not registered, and not renderable)

`stable_worldmodel/envs/image_positioning.py` exists in the source tree and its `register(...)` call
is **commented out**, so it is absent from the live registry and `gym.make` cannot reach it. It also
takes a required `images` argument with no default, so there is nothing to construct. Reading the
directory instead of the registry is how this env gets onto a coverage list it does not belong on.

Even so it is worth a verdict, because it fails two gates outright:

* **G1.** `reset()` builds the background from `np.random.randn(...)` — the **global, unseeded**
  numpy RNG, not `self.np_random` — FFTs it, applies a `1/f^p` filter and inverts it. The frame
  therefore depends on process-wide random state that no observation records, and two resets with the
  same seed give different pictures.
* **G3.** The decoder is a list of **user-supplied PIL images**, bordered and converted per instance;
  the env's own `__main__` fetches them from Google image URLs. The state→pixel map is not a constant
  of the family, it is whatever the caller passed in.

**Verdict: NO-GO, on G1 and G3.** This is the ALE failure mode rather than the RoboCasa one: the
frame is not a function of any state the env exposes.

## OGBench cube and scene

`swm/OGBCube-v0` and `swm/OGBScene-v0` construct but need EGL to render (they default to GLFW and
raise `X11: The DISPLAY environment variable is missing` on a headless node). stable-envs covers
OGBench's **mazes** — 25 cells, interior 100% exact — and not its manipulation scenes. Their
variation spaces (`agent.ee_start_position`, `cube.size`, `cube.goal_yaw`, `camera.angle_delta`,
`light.intensity`, and `lock_color` for the scene env) say why they are a different problem: a
movable camera and a light are part of the appearance, which the maze plate-bake explicitly assumes
away.

**Not gated in this pass.** Recorded as a gap rather than guessed at. The cheap first measurement is
the same one that settled RoboCasa in twenty seconds: reset twice and compare `ngeom`/`nmesh`.

## What this changes about the criterion

Nothing — which is the point of having one. Every verdict above falls out of the four gates in
[`when_does_this_pay_off.md`](when_does_this_pay_off.md) applied unchanged, and the two surprises
point the same way the earlier ones did:

* **Fetch clears all four gates and is still not built.** The criterion says whether the approach
  *pays*; it does not say whether the renderer *exists*. Keeping those separate is what lets the
  Fetch entry be a work item with a measurement attached rather than a rejection.
* **The visually simplest env in this note was the hardest to make exact.** `TwoRoom` draws a
  rectangle and two blurry dots, and it took a float64 `exp`, an intermediate uint8 quantisation and
  a 41-pixel Gaussian tail to reach interior parity. Visual complexity is not the axis — this is the
  same lesson OGBench's articulated humanoid taught from the other direction.
