## The conclusion

**43 of 49 render-distinct configurations pass Gate A outright, 1 on a documented subset, 5 fail and 0 could not be reached.** All 45 of 45 parity configurations meet the interior-exactness contract **each one declares**, and those contracts are not identical: 26 are bit-exact on every interior pixel at the shared floor of `1.0`, 16 hold to a lower floor their class declares and explains, and 3 are on scenes where the official renderer leaves no interior pixel to test at all. Not one of the failures below is a parity comparison.

5 configurations were **already failing before any optimization**, and they fail
for two unrelated reasons. Three are visual-tier DM Control renderers that no longer meet the
fidelity numbers their own classes declare — on two independent metrics, with the residual
concentrated in the skybox; see *The visual-tier drift*. Two are parity-tier OGBench cells that
pass their parity comparison and fail a MANDATORY check instead: a fresh env handed the stored
`(state, style)` draws a frame 1/255 different, so for those two cells the frame depends on
something a dataset would not have recorded. None of this was fixed here. A renderer changed
before its baseline is frozen has no baseline, and the value of the finding is precisely that
it is now dated to BEFORE any Step 6 work.

Every configuration that was reached now has a frozen state corpus, a frame digest and a metric
beside it, so a Step 6 rewrite can be replayed on the identical states and the question "was
this already broken?" has an answer instead of an opinion.

### The backend-agreement check cannot reach the 3-D families

`check_backend_agreement` is `not_applicable` on 47 configuration(s) for
one reason, and it is worth stating plainly rather than leaving as a status code. The check
compares the fused Triton rasterizer against the readable torch implementation of the
**same** `Scene2D`; the mjwarp, raycast3d and nvdiff families do not build a `Scene2D` at
all, so it returns `env renders without a Scene2D`. Their classes also do not offer two
interchangeable backends — `DMControlSuite` bakes a plate and ray-casts the movers,
`DMControlTrackCom` hands the whole scene to MJWarp, and an env is one or the other — so
there is no second implementation of one picture to compare against.

This is a gap in the CHECK SUITE, not a defect in a renderer, and Step 4 does not close it:
writing a new cross-backend comparison would be building the second correctness framework
section 1 forbids. It is recorded because "not applicable" on a check section 4.1 lists as
family-specific should be a fact somebody chose, not a status nobody read.

## The representation tripwire

`A = dino_cos(ours, official) / dino_cos(blank, official)`, against the bound Step 2 froze: `parity` ≤ 0.1, `visual` ≤ 0.25.

Diagnostic, and deliberately weak on its own: a cosine distance is not a task success rate and
this page does not treat it as one. It is here because it is the cheapest thing that notices a
Step 6 change that keeps every pixel metric inside its bound and still moves what the frame
means to an encoder. `ours / jpeg95` below 1 means regenerating the frame from its state lands
closer to the official renderer than storing that renderer's output as JPEG and reading it back.

## The frozen state corpora

The artifact Step 6 replays. Each corpus is drawn from the env's **own** reset-and-rollout
distribution — so every state is one the env genuinely reaches — and then selected for coverage:
the most typical row, then up to half the budget on per-dimension extremes taken widest
dimension first, then the rest by farthest-point spread. Deterministic given the seed; no
generator is consulted by the selection. Synthesising extremes instead would have produced
states the env never reaches, and a parity number on an unreachable pose describes nothing.

The APPEARANCE is frozen with them. The checks that take states are fed a two-group style from
`conformance.uniform_style(seed=0)`, whose digest is recorded beside the frames, so the
variation axis a Step 6 rerun replays is the same one measured here — and the parity and
oracle numbers are taken at the official default appearance, which is what parity is defined
against.

**It is not the state draw.** Each declaration records the spread of `mean_abs` over three
independent 64-state draws, and those spreads are tight — a few hundredths for cheetah and
hopper. The measurements above are outside them by whole units, which is not something a
different draw of the same distribution produces.

**It is not the whole stack either.** `DMCHumanoidStand` reproduces the declared measurement almost
exactly on this same interpreter, well inside its own recorded draw spread.
And `DMCHumanoidStand` is the only visual configuration with **no skybox in frame at all** — every configuration that drifted has skybox in frame, and the one that did not, does not.

**What the pictures show.** In `comparisons/DMCWalkerWalk.png` the official sky is a smooth
gradient and ours is covered in white specks, and the amplified difference panel is almost
entirely that speckle field. The mechanism is the one `stable_envs/envs/dmcontrol.py`
already documents: the skybox is an 800x4800 gradient with `mark="random"` specks, dm_control
MINIFIES it and averages the specks away, and MJWarp casts one ray per pixel and paints them
at full contrast. What changed is the SIZE of that disagreement, not its nature.

**What differs between the declaration and this run**, for whoever picks this up in Step 6:

```
declared at  84 px, 64 states per domain from sample_states (the domain's own initialize_episode plus a 0-40 step random-action rollout), default appearance, H200 / driver 590.48.01 / MUJOCO_GL=egl, mujoco 3.11.0 + mujoco-warp 3.11.0, against dm_control's physics.render at camera 0
measured at  mujoco 3.12.0 + mujoco-warp 3.12.0 + warp 1.17.0, RTX 3090
```

That is a difference, not a diagnosis. **Nothing here was changed to make these pass** —
section 18 — and neither the thresholds nor the renderers were touched. What this baseline
buys is that the drift is now dated and attributable: it exists BEFORE any Step 6
optimization, so a rewrite of these renderers cannot be blamed for it and cannot hide behind
it either.

## What Step 4 does not establish

That a world model or a controller cannot tell the renderers apart. Gate A says the renderer is
internally coherent, that its fidelity declaration is supported by a measurement, that the
configuration it draws is the one the swm id registers, and that its output is reproducible.
The semantic claim is Step 5's, on PushT and Reacher, and on a visual-tier arm if a visual-tier
claim is to be advertised. Nothing here licenses it.

It also establishes nothing about speed, in either direction. Step 3 found GPU rendering broadly
healthy and CPU rendering broadly poor, with 16 configurations having no CPU renderer at all;
none of that is evidence about what the pixels are, and this page deliberately does not repeat it.
