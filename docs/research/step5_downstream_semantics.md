## Reacher + DINO-WM

### The corpus

`dmc/reacher_random.h5` — 10000 episodes, 2010000 rows, columns `action`, `ep_idx`, `finger_pos`, `id`, `observation`, `pixels`, `qpos`, `qvel`, `render_time`, `reward`, `score`, `step_idx`, `success`, `target_pos`, `terminated`, `truncated`.

### Do the two renderers draw the same reacher?

The pre-flight, and on this environment it is not a formality. `stable_worldmodel`'s
reacher is not `suite.load('reacher', 'hard')`: it recompiles the model through `mjcf`
after editing materials and geom types, and its variation default `render_target = 0`
sets the target material's **alpha** to zero. `stable_envs`' DM Control appearance space
carries material colour, light diffuse and camera fovy and has no alpha, so if the
reference declines to draw its target and we draw it, every frame differs by a red disc
and the downstream comparison measures that disc.

Measured, by moving the target geom and re-rendering the reference: moving it changes at
most **0/255**, so the reference **does NOT draw its target**.

| our target placement | all exact | interior exact | mean \|d\| | max \|d\| | SSIM   |
|----------------------|-----------|----------------|------------|-----------|--------|
| as_placed            | 0.9841    | 0.999867       | 0.459      | 216       | 0.9970 |
| offscreen            | 0.9859    | 0.999867       | 0.171      | 116       | 0.9996 |

The harness uses `offscreen`.

#### The gate, applied to the floor

Running the frozen rule over those three random-policy arms is worth doing once, because
it is the case where agreement between renderers means nothing at all -- a policy that
ignores the pixels agrees with itself whatever is drawn. The rule says so:

```
official     4/50
stable-envs  3/50
paired       delta -2.00 pts, CI [-12.37, 8.37], McNemar p = 1.000
control      +10.0 pts, p = 0.23  ->  degraded = false
VERDICT      INCONCLUSIVE
```

A p of 1.000 on the paired difference, and the verdict is still not a pass. That is the
control requirement doing the only job it has: the blank arm scored ABOVE official here,
because with random actions the frames are irrelevant, and an evaluation that cannot be
hurt by removing the picture cannot be used to argue that two pictures are equivalent.

### The corpus does not match the environment that published it

Found while diagnosing a 30-point drop, and it changes how the two STORED pixel surfaces
have to be read. The planner's first observation and its goal image are not rendered at
evaluation time in the official arm: they are read off disk from the corpus. Our arm
regenerates them from the `qpos` stored beside each frame, which is the repository's own
thesis -- and the thesis is testable, so it was tested.

On 32 corpus rows, every re-render of a stored state compared against
the stored frame itself:

| re-render of the stored state | all exact | mean \|d\| | max \|d\| | SSIM   |
|-------------------------------|-----------|------------|-----------|--------|
| ours_as_placed                | 0.5013    | 18.699     | 217       | 0.8869 |
| ours_offscreen                | 0.5021    | 18.474     | 115       | 0.8987 |
| official_default              | 0.5148    | 18.316     | 48        | 0.9000 |
| official_target_on            | 0.5138    | 18.536     | 217       | 0.8883 |

**MuJoCo cannot reproduce the corpus either.** `official_default` is the live environment
rendering the same `qpos` and target position the frame was stored with, and it lands
18.3/255 away with barely half its pixels exact. Ours lands at 18.5. The contact sheet
(`corpus_probe.png`) shows the mechanism: the stored frames have a CHECKERBOARD floor and
no current render draws one. The environment's appearance drifted after the data was
published, and the signature is unmistakable -- half the pixels exact, nothing off by
more than 48, which is one set of checker squares and nothing else.

Two consequences. Regenerating a stored frame moves the model off its training
distribution no matter which renderer regenerates it, so a downstream arm that swaps the
stored surfaces is measuring the drift and not the renderer. And a *live*-surface
comparison is unaffected, because both arms render the same environment as it is today.

## What Step 5 does not establish

That every renderer in the library is semantically equivalent. Two environments were measured,
chosen in Step 2 before any of this was run, and they are parity-tier. A visual-tier renderer
is a different render of the same scene by construction, and nothing here licenses a claim
about one.

It also establishes nothing about speed or about pixel exactness. Step 3 measured the first
and Step 4 the second; a downstream success rate is a third thing, and the reason to measure
it is precisely that neither of the other two implies it.

## Issues discovered

Recorded, not repaired -- except where a repair was the only way to run the experiment at
all, in which case what was changed and why is beside it.

1. **stable_worldmodel's reacher hides its target; our renderer cannot express that.** `render_target = 0` sets the target material's alpha to zero. `stable_envs`' DM Control appearance space carries material colour, light diffuse and camera fovy and has no alpha, so the only way this renderer can decline to paint a geom is to put it outside the camera frustum. Measured, not assumed: moving the reference's target changes 0/255. The harness runs `offscreen` and records it as part of the arm. A DM Control visibility knob is Step 6 work. (`examples/results/step5/reacher/parity_probe.json`)
2. **interior-pixel exactness is blind to the target.** The two target placements differ by a visible disc -- mean |d| 0.459 against 0.171 -- and their interior-exact fractions are identical to sixteen digits. `interior_mask` keeps only pixels whose 3x3 neighbourhood in the reference is flat, and the disc lands on a textured checkerboard floor, so every pixel it changes is already excluded. A whole object appears and the interior metric does not move. Step 4's parity numbers should be read with that in mind. (`examples/results/step5/reacher/parity_probe.json`)
3. **the DINO-WM Reacher checkpoint behind the recorded 79% does not exist.** Not in either checkout, not in `$STABLEWM_HOME`, not on the cluster, not in the HuggingFace orgs that publish this project's weights. `docs/baselines.md` lists the checkpoint column as `NA` for every DINO-WM row. Step 5 trained a replacement with the repository's own script. (`docs/research/step5_downstream_semantics.md`)
4. **the committed DINO-WM training config cannot instantiate.** `scripts/train/config/prejepa.yaml` names `stable_worldmodel.wm.prejepa.CausalPredictor`. The class lives in `...prejepa.module` and that package's `__init__` re-exports only from `.prejepa`, so hydra raises `InstantiationException` before the dataset is opened. One line, and the same file already spells its two neighbours correctly. (`scratch/step5/train_config/prejepa_reacher.yaml`)
5. **the training script names a callback no released version ships.** `scripts/train/prejepa.py` builds its trainer with `spt.callbacks.CPUOffloadCallback()`, which is absent from `stable-pretraining==0.1.8` -- the version stable-worldmodel's own `uv.lock` pins. Thirty-odd callbacks are exported and none offloads anything. Step 5 supplies a no-op stand-in rather than editing their repository. (`scratch/step5/train_prejepa_shim.py`)
6. **the published LeWM Reacher checkpoint cannot be loaded under transformers 5.x.** The weights carry `encoder.encoder.layer.N.attention.attention.query.*`; the instantiated model wants `encoder.layers.N.attention.q_proj.*`. HuggingFace renamed ViT parameters between major versions and the checkpoint predates the rename. Remapped rather than abandoned, under guards: the mapping must be a bijection onto the state dict of a model built from the checkpoint's own config, every shape equal, nothing unmatched on either side, and a strict load. 303 tensors, 192 renamed, 111 untouched; no tensor was modified, only keys. Those guards rule out a STRUCTURAL error and cannot rule out a semantic one -- `q_proj` and `k_proj` are both (192, 192) and a swap between them satisfies every check -- so the remap is verified against the task instead, where the checkpoint has a recorded 86% and a random policy scored 8% on the same episodes. (`scratch/step5/remap_lewm.py`)
7. **the corpus and the environment that published it no longer agree.** Every re-render of a stored reacher state -- MuJoCo's included -- sits ~18/255 from the stored frame, with barely half its pixels exact and nothing off by more than 48. The stored frames have a checkerboard floor and no current render draws one. Consequence for anyone regenerating this corpus: the drift costs a planner 28 points with stable-envs nowhere in the arm, so a downstream comparison that swaps the STORED surfaces measures the drift, not the renderer. The live surface is unaffected, because both arms render today's environment. (`examples/results/step5/reacher/corpus_probe.json`)
8. **planning with a patch-level world model costs hours per 50 episodes, and cannot be batched.** CEM scores 300 candidate plans per iteration, and DINO-WM's predictor runs over 768 tokens (3 history frames x 256 DINOv2 patches) with 16 heads. The attention score tensor is 300 x 16 x 768 x 768 = 2.83e9 entries, 10.55 GiB in fp32 -- four times every other activation in the layer combined, and materialised because the predictor passes an explicit boolean mask which makes SDPA fall back to its math backend. Measured consequences on a 24 GB 3090: `solver.batch_size` above 2 runs out of memory (5, 10 and 50 all fail, each holding ~18 GiB before the allocation that tips it), so the committed default of 1 is a memory constraint rather than an oversight; `bf16` cannot be used at all because `PreJEPA.encode` hard-casts its input with `.float()`, so casting the backbone raises a dtype mismatch; and PyTorch leaves TF32 matmul off by default, so the whole planner runs in full fp32 on a card whose tensor cores are idle. None of this is gradient retention -- `solve` is `@torch.inference_mode()`, `get_action` adds `no_grad`, and the model is `requires_grad_(False)`. LeWM runs the identical solver in 77 seconds because its predictor sees 3 pooled tokens instead of 768, a score tensor of 0.16 MiB. (`scratch/step5/tf32_probe.sbatch`)
9. **Step 2's recorded PushT control degradation is the unpaired figure.** The frozen spec records `control.delta_points = -84.0`, which is the 300-episode official rate minus the 50-episode blank rate. Paired on the seed the control actually ran, it is -92.0. Both clear the -20 requirement by a wide margin; the artifact is what this page reports. (`examples/results/step5/pusht/gate_h.json`)
